# Abstract

Autonomous UAV swarms promise rapid, large‑scale coverage for Search‑and‑Rescue missions, yet their operational reliability hinges on fault‑tolerant coordination that can survive individual drone failures. This paper introduces the Intelligent Replanning Drone Swarm (IRDS), a fully decentralized architecture that preserves mission continuity without a central controller. IRDS combines a gossip‑based consensus protocol—"Rumour Mill"—with a market‑driven task allocation mechanism to detect degraded agents, reach distributed agreement, and dynamically reassign search sectors. The approach is engineered under aerospace safety standards (ARP4761A, JARUS SORA) and formally verified using UPPAAL model checking to guarantee consensus and safety properties. Extensive fault‑injection experiments in a high‑fidelity PyBullet simulation demonstrate that the swarm reliably identifies failures, maintains consensus under intermittent communication, and reallocates tasks, achieving over 95 % mission success in resource‑constrained scenarios. The results provide a verifiable, self‑healing framework for dependable swarm coordination.
