# Abstract

Autonomous UAV swarms promise rapid, large‑scale coverage for Search‑and‑Rescue missions, yet their operational reliability hinges on fault‑tolerant coordination that can survive individual drone failures. This paper introduces the Intelligent Replanning Drone Swarm (IRDS), a resilient architecture that preserves mission continuity under agent degradation. IRDS employs a market‑driven task allocation mechanism coupled with a multi-agent verification protocol to detect degraded agents, reach consensus, and dynamically reassign search sectors. The approach is engineered under aerospace safety standards (ARP4761A, JARUS SORA) and evaluated to guarantee task coverage and safety properties. Extensive fault‑injection experiments in a high‑fidelity PyBullet simulation demonstrate that the swarm reliably identifies failures, maintains consensus, and reallocates tasks, achieving over 95 % mission success in resource‑constrained scenarios. The results provide a verifiable, self‑healing framework for dependable swarm coordination.
