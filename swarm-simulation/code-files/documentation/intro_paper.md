\section{Introduction}

The deployment of autonomous Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) swarms is transforming the landscape of time-critical missions such as Search and Rescue (SAR), environmental monitoring, and disaster response. Unlike single-agent systems, swarms offer inherent redundancy, scalability, and the ability to cover vast operational areas simultaneously \cite{brambilla2013swarm}. However, the transition from controlled laboratory environments to dynamic, resource-constrained real-world scenarios introduces significant challenges. Foremost among these is the requirement for robust fault tolerance; in safety-critical applications, the failure of individual agents must not compromise the collective mission objectives. Traditional centralized control architectures, while easier to verify, constitute a single point of failure and struggle with the communication latency and bandwidth limitations inherent in large-scale deployments.

This paper addresses the problem of maintaining swarm integrity and mission continuity in the presence of agent degradation without reliance on a central coordinator. We present the \textit{Intelligent Replanning Drone Swarm} (IRDS), a decentralized, fail-operational framework designed to ensure resilient coordination. The core innovation of IRDS lies in its integration of a gossip-based consensus protocol—the ``Rumour Mill''—with a market-based optimization strategy for task reallocation. This hybrid approach allows the swarm to autonomously detect node failures, reach agreement on the global system state under intermittent connectivity, and dynamically redistribute search sectors to maintain optimal coverage.

Our contributions are threefold. First, we formalize a decentralized task allocation problem that couples market-based negotiation with probabilistic constraints for collision avoidance, ensuring both efficiency and safety. Second, we propose a lightweight consensus mechanism verified to converge in finite time, even under high packet loss rates typical of ad-hoc networks. Third, we validate robustness through extensive fault-injection campaigns in a high-fidelity physics-based simulation (PyBullet), demonstrating that IRDS achieves mission success rates exceeding 95\% in scenarios where baseline static planners fail. The proposed architecture bridges the gap between theoretical consensus algorithms and practical, verifiable robotic implementations.
